Points of diversion

What this is: live data from the New Mexico Office of the State Engineer's Points of Diversion layer — 279,991 records statewide, current as of September 2026 — pulled through this project's own backend. Field codes are decoded using OSE's own published data dictionary, so a status of "DCL" reads as "Declaration" and a county of "BE" as "Bernalillo" in the agency's own wording.

What this isn't: a complete record, and the OSE says so itself. Its own description of this layer states the data "are not complete, not all locations have been recorded in spatial data, and an exhaustive search can only be done by the NMOSE themselves." So nothing found here is proof nothing exists — for a definitive answer, go to the OSE directly. New Mexico also records points of diversion rather than place-of-use areas, so unlike Idaho or Nevada there's no shape showing which ground a right actually serves.

Also worth knowing: a well depth or water level of 0 in these records means "not recorded", not "at the surface", so those are shown as unknown rather than as a misleading zero. Parcel ownership and a separate well-construction dataset haven't been researched for New Mexico yet.

Sources: services2.arcgis.com (OSE Points of Diversion, via the OSE's own open-data hub) · nmwrrs.ose.nm.gov (official records) · geocoding.geo.census.gov (geocoding only) · OpenStreetMap (map tiles)
