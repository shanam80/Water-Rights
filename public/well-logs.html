Which wells near this property have logs on file — and how do I read them?

A well log is the record of what a drilling crew found on the way down. This is subsurface oil and gas data, not water rights — a separate research tool that happens to describe the same ground. Nothing here affects or reflects the water coverage on the state pages.

What this is: live public records — the Texas Railroad Commission's own map service (its Well Logs layer, one row per log document) and New Mexico's OCD well layer plus its imaging system — pulled through this project's backend. No login, and nothing here is sample data.

What this isn't: water rights. Oil and gas wells are a different regulatory world from the water coverage on the state pages, and the two are deliberately never mixed. A log tells you what rock and fluids a crew logged on the way down; it is not a statement about groundwater availability, ownership, or your right to use any of it.

Sources: gis.rrc.texas.gov (Texas well locations & logs) · mapservice.nmstatelands.org (NMOCD wells) · ocdimage.emnrd.nm.gov (New Mexico log images) · geocoding.geo.census.gov (geocoding only) · OpenStreetMap (map tiles)
