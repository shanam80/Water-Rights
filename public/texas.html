Texas Groundwater & Well Lookup

Can you drill here, and what are the rules? Find out in seconds — free, and without making an account.

Texas works fundamentally differently from every other state here. Groundwater follows the "rule of capture," not prior appropriation — there's no state-tracked "water right" with a priority date the way Colorado, Idaho, Utah, Montana, or Nevada have. Ownership is simply an incident of owning the land above it. So instead of water rights, this looks up what is genuinely tracked in Texas: the parcel, which Groundwater Conservation District (if any) regulates it, and nearby wells on file.

Landowners & ranchers

Texas has no state water right to look up — what matters is your district's rules. See spacing, production limits and permit thresholds where they apply.

What this is: live data from TWDB's Groundwater Database (well locations), TWDB's Groundwater Conservation District boundaries — browsable as a statewide map above — and, for 90 of the 101 districts, their actual spacing/production/permitting rules, TWDB's automated groundwater-level monitoring network, and TxGIO's StratMap statewide parcel dataset — pulled through this project's own backend.

What this isn't yet: per-well production/yield. TWDB's live map data only includes each well's location, depth, owner, use, and aquifer; the monitoring-well water levels above are real but regional, not specific to any one well or property. The fuller per-well construction/yield detail exists in TWDB's records but is served through a legacy report system this project can't reach directly yet — search a well's State Well Number at TWDB's own reports page (linked on each well card) for that detail in the meantime.

Sources: services.twdb.texas.gov (TWDB wells, GCD boundaries) · waterdatafortexas.org (groundwater monitoring) · StratMap/TxGIO (parcels) · geocoding.geo.census.gov (geocoding only) · OpenStreetMap (map tiles)
