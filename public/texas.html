Texas Groundwater & Well Lookup

Texas works fundamentally differently from every other state here. Groundwater follows the "rule of capture," not prior appropriation — there's no state-tracked "water right" with a priority date the way Colorado, Idaho, Utah, Montana, or Nevada have. Ownership is simply an incident of owning the land above it. So instead of water rights, this looks up what is genuinely tracked in Texas: the parcel, which Groundwater Conservation District (if any) regulates it, and nearby wells on file.

Nearby wells

What this is: live data from TWDB's Groundwater Database (well locations), TWDB's Groundwater Conservation District boundaries, and TxGIO's StratMap statewide parcel dataset — pulled through this project's own backend.

What this isn't yet: well yield or water-level readings. TWDB's live map data only includes each well's location, depth, owner, use, and aquifer — the fuller construction/yield detail exists in TWDB's records but is served through a legacy report system this project can't reach directly yet. Search a well's State Well Number at TWDB's own reports page (linked below) for that detail in the meantime. This also isn't a district's actual production/spacing rules — only which district applies, not its specific restrictions (a harder, separate piece not built yet).

Sources: services.twdb.texas.gov (TWDB wells, GCD boundaries) · StratMap/TxGIO (parcels) · geocoding.geo.census.gov (geocoding only) · OpenStreetMap (map tiles)
